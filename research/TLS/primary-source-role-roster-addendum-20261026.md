## What the named product represents

The retained administrative metadata row `data/administrative-sources.json` identifies `gb:TLS:ADM2` / `TLS-ADM2-22284137`, canonical type “Administrative Posts,” source “WHO, OCHA ROAP,” represented year 2020, 65 source units, and a source-level CC BY 3.0 IGO license declaration. It separately records a 19 January 2023 `sourceDataUpdateDate` and 12 December 2023 geoBoundaries `buildDate`; neither is an effective-boundary date. The retained original-source attribution/sidecar evidence and historical issue #302 were summarized in `source-attribution-use-draft.md`; they are metadata and provenance, not proof of adoption or exact feature ancestry.

The official OCHA IM Toolbox describes COD-ABs as administrative boundary/geographic reference layers linked to gazetteers and P-codes, adopted by a country or regional Information Management Working Group, and subject to annual review. It says the review checks for administrative change, errors, newly discovered data, or no changes, and notes HDX metadata normally records the date range during which boundaries represent the established (often legislated) reality. That describes the intended operational standard and its maintenance process, not a claim that this specific TLS layer was legally adopted or was reviewed in a particular year. The HDX indexed entry for **Timor-Leste – Subnational Administrative Boundaries** identifies COD-AB version 01 and OCHA ROAP as contributor; direct opening of that exact HDX record returned HTTP 403 Forbidden, so no further access attempt was made.

WHO's country-specific 2015–2019 Cooperation Strategy documents health staff serving at district and administrative-post levels and calls for post-level strengthening; its population and service statistics are from earlier years. WHO's Timor-Leste 2021–2025 malaria prevention strategy, published in 2023, is closer to the product era: it states that the malaria programme operates through municipality, administrative-post/sub-district and health-facility levels; post-level malaria officers are attached to community health centres, conduct prevention work, and report monthly to municipal officers. These primary documents support an operational public-health/service and reporting role for administrative posts. They do not say that WHO or OCHA drew each polygon, identify the exact geometry used by a particular programme, or supply lineage from a post/health catchment to any of the 22 source feature IDs.

## The 2023 laws and the direct Atauro row

Two official 2023 laws show why the 2020 source year cannot be treated as a current structure certificate:

* **Law No. 14/2023**, published 24 May 2023, created Loes by splitting Maubara and Quelicai Antigo and Matebian by splitting Quelicai; it took effect 1 January 2024. Its amended Baucau and Liquiçá provisions state that territorial areas are approved by a separate Government instrument. The law is structural/legal evidence, not a map or exact boundary schedule. The 22-owner/31-feature roster here contains no Maubara, Quelicai, Loes, Quelicai Antigo, Matebian, Baucau or Liquiçá feature; therefore those specific splits are a dataset-wide vintage issue, not a demonstrated direct change to one of these 22 owner-feature rows.
* **Law No. 19/2023**, published 5 December 2023 and effective the next day, extinguished the administrative circumscription that corresponded to the then municipality of Ataúro and established Ataúro as a first-tier circumscription covering the entire island. The law expressly says administrative posts are below municipalities and exist to bring public services closer to populations; it also assigns government responsibility for maintaining the official administrative chart. This is directly relevant to the package's owner 42171 / source feature `gb:TLS:ADM2:22284137B11289182458696` (“Atauro”): the feature is part of the 2020 second-tier post dataset, while the 2023 legal structure makes Ataúro itself a first-tier unit. The law identifies the whole island but does not provide coordinates or prove the exact lineage/geometric extent of the 2020 Atauro polygon. This is a specific temporal/semantic mismatch lead, not a geometry result or automatic disposition.

The Ministry of State Administration’s 2024 activity report says Law 14/2023 created the three new second-tier posts and that Ministerial Diploma 40/2023 established their administrations; it describes post administrations as municipal-authority extension services at the territorial level of the post. This corroborates the administrative-service function and implementation timeline; it is not an exact map. For locations other than Atauro, the 2023 legal change is context only unless an exact roster row and applicable legal provision show direct incidence.

## Finding and limits

The best-supported source-role description is: the 2020 GeoBoundaries TLS ADM2 asset represents administrative posts and names WHO/OCHA ROAP; the OCHA COD-AB framework is an operational humanitarian reference process, and WHO evidence confirms posts were used as health-service/reporting levels. These sources do not establish that this specific geometry was adopted by the government or that its 22 selected feature shapes were constructed for WHO's operational boundaries. The exact package roster connects 22 indexed owners to 22 named TLS ADM2 source IDs and keeps eight TLS ADM1 plus one Indonesia ADM1 competitor distinct.

Temporal follow-up is narrow and actionable: owner 42171 Atauro has an express 2023 first-tier legal change; source-layer status should be interpreted against it. Law 14/2023 changed other posts outside this package's 22-owner row set, while demonstrating that post areas may require separate Government instruments. No specific owner-level instrument/map was sought for the remaining 21 owners. The exact consumed 2020 feature bodies are retained in the package corpus, but this addendum does not inspect geometry or prove any source feature's ancestry, effective extent, or land/water fit. No owner, disposition, geometry, or credit was changed.
