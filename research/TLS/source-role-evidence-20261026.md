## Later official administrative context

Timor-Leste Law No. 14/2023, published in the [Jornal da República, Series I, No. 19, Supplement I](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2023/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_19_SUPLEMENTO_I.pdf), is a later legal change relevant to interpreting a 2020 “Administrative Posts” layer. The law is dated 24 May 2023 and took effect 1 January 2024. It amended the administrative-post lists in Baucau and Liquiçá, including creation of Matebian, Quelicai Antigo, and Loes by splitting existing posts. The law’s amended provisions state that the territorial areas are to be approved by a separate Government instrument; the law itself does not supply owner-level map schedules or exact boundary geometry. Its preamble describes the administrative restructuring and transition, rather than certifying any prior dataset feature.

The official [INETL 2022 Population and Housing Census Main Report](https://inetl-ip.gov.tl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Final-Main-Report_TLPHC-Census_18052023-1.pdf), published in May 2023, reports a census framework of 67 administrative posts, 452 sucos, and 2,231 aldeias. This is statistical/census use of administrative units. The report’s 67-post figure differs from the 65-feature count in the retained 2020 source record, but the difference alone does not identify which features changed or establish a one-to-one correspondence. The report predates the January 2024 effective date of Law 14/2023.

## Assessment and limit

The evidence supports describing the retained GeoBoundaries source as a 2020 administrative-post boundary dataset, with a historical source-specific CC BY 3.0 IGO declaration. It is temporally older than the 2023 restructuring effective in 2024, and the later law expressly leaves exact territorial areas to separate Government instruments. The captured source body is missing from the checked local directory, and neither issue #302 nor the retained inventory provides the legal area instruments or a 31-feature-to-owner lineage. No conclusion is made that the 2020 boundaries are invalid, that every owner changed, or that the exact 31 source features are equivalent to a current legal map.

The next decision-changing step, if needed for a particular owner, is to identify the applicable Government instrument approving that post’s territory and compare its dated schedule/map to the retained source feature identity. This is a bounded owner-specific follow-up, not a universal prerequisite or a claim that no such instrument exists.
