## 29 April 1887 protocol: actual official text

BOEIC1888 printed pp242–243 = PDF pp244–245, visually read in full from the protocol heading to signatures. The preceding part of printed242 belongs to the separate Manyanga protocol; it was not conflated with the Ubangi instrument. The succeeding printed244/PDF246 begins an unrelated administrative-district decree.

The protocol makes the Ubangi thalweg the frontier from its Congo confluence to the intersection with 4° north. North of that parallel, the Free State undertakes no political action on the right bank, and France undertakes none on the left bank, with the thalweg separating them. A further paragraph says the Free State's northern boundary must not fall below 4° north. The text refers to the final paragraphs of Article2 of the 5 February1885 convention and separately invokes Article5 for the northern limit. It is signed at Brussels on29 April1887 by Van Eetvelde and Bourée.

No island-specific schedule, coordinate series, or attached map is identified in this protocol text or its immediate page continuation. This is a bounded reading, not a claim that no map exists elsewhere.

## 14 August 1894 arrangement: actual official text

BOEIC1894 printed pp254–257 = PDF pp260–263. All six articles and signature/ratification note were read in extracted text; printed pp255–257 were visually checked. Printed254 starts with the separate Anglo-Congolese withdrawal declaration before the French arrangement heading.

Article1 follows the Ubangi thalweg to the M’Bomou/Ouellé confluence, then the M’Bomou thalweg to its source, a straight line to the Congo–Nile watershed, and that divide to 30° east of Greenwich (27°40′ Paris).

**The original Article1 footnote explicitly defines the names M’Bomou and Sources du M’Bomou by the indications in Junker's map, Gotha, Justus Perthes,1888.** This is a concrete source reference controlling which historical river/source the text means. It does not provide a modern coordinate transformation. The same reference is repeated in the ensuing neutrality declaration at printed260/PDF266. No map is inserted within the treaty's printed254–257 sequence; the reference is to an external published map.

Article2 provides French policing on the M’Bomou under conditions to be set by a special arrangement, with narrowly limited pursuit and passage on the left bank. It is not a separate left-bank territorial grant. Article3 calls for handover of Free State posts north of the stipulated boundary as accredited French agents arrive, with concerted instructions. Article4 limits further occupation/political action west and north of the described 30°E/5°30′N line toward the Nile. Articles5–6 concern ratification and signatures.

The arrangement is dated Paris,14 August1894. The closing official note states ratifications exchanged at Paris on **27 December1894**. The ensuing neutrality declaration is dated **28 December1894** at printed263/PDF269. These are separately labeled events and must not be collapsed into a single supposed contradiction.
