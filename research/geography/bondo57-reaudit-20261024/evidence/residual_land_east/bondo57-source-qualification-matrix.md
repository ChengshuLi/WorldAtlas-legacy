The UN disclaimer withholds endorsement; it is not evidence of positional error.

## What the thalweg rule can establish

An administrative area can share part of its perimeter with an international river frontier. A treaty's thalweg rule may therefore govern that portion even when the administrative polygon's source does not state a channel convention. The remainder of the administrative perimeter need not be a thalweg.

The missing convention prevents us from demonstrating that this particular vector implements the rule; it does not demonstrate that the vector violates it. Likewise, the COD target archive's watercourse splitting and topology checks do not identify its atlas edition, current channel, or treaty implementation. Choosing COD merely because its represented date is later would be unsupported.

Reasonable qualification can rest on sufficiently clear, source-specific evidence relevant to the actual contact. It does not require an impossible sovereignty certificate, a prescribed new constructor, or endless searching. Conversely, polygon names, licence matches and broad map labels do not qualify a contested edge merely because they are authentic.
