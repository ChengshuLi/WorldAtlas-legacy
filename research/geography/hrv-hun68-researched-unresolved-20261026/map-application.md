## Application work and result

I recomputed WGS84 center-coordinate extents from the exact 68 keys using the already-pinned WorldAtlas pixel-centre method (262,166 grid; no geometry input or producer run). The three bounded extents are: owner22086, 3 cells; owner22101, 34 cells; owner22186, 31 cells. No coordinates were transmitted externally. The index/map review establishes B-sector relevance at regional scale, especially the Kotoriba B11–16 sheets, but not a cell-to-sheet association at the required precision.

I checked the map scans for marginalia and the identified projection paper for axis/frame controls. The scans show projected rectangular coordinate ticks, map-sheet IDs, marker IDs and 1:5,000 scale; they do not show latitude/longitude graticules or geospatial PDF metadata. The paper provides a candidate projection/datum family and a national transformation accuracy summary, but the retained pages do not provide a verifiable local control-point set or residual model tying the Pula table coordinate convention and scanned B/C sheet raster to the exact WorldAtlas WGS84 center coordinates. The Pula tables have sparse coordinate entries, rather than coordinates for every line point; the B12 markers directly relevant to the Mura description have no complete line vertex list in the retained table.

An exploratory generic transverse-Mercator calculation was deliberately not used: it is not the documented Laborde method, does not establish axis convention, and its output cannot be validated against map-sheet extents or local controls. No side-of-line result is reported. In particular, I did not use the historic polygon/modern Croatian administrative geometry as a control, since that would be circular. The national 2m/5m datum-transform figures are not a registration error estimate for the scanned border maps. The map-grid ticks could support scan-fit residual measurement after correct CRS is fixed, but without numeric `alpha_c` no valid cell-center-to-grid conversion or combined transform/scan/line-width uncertainty can be calculated. At 1:5,000, the later application would also need direct measurement of raster scale/scan residuals and line width before even a conservative near-line classification.

## Qualification boundary / next deciding input

The evidence is sufficient to say the official source is relevant and that B11–16/B12 furnish a concrete neighboring map-series context for the Kotoriba part. It is not sufficient to assign any exact one of the 68 keys to a specific sheet/marker, project the Pula/B/C line into the current grid, or claim inside/outside/contact or historic side. The specific missing deciding parameter is the numeric Laborde central-line azimuth/axis convention for the map coordinate system (or an authoritative equivalent that uniquely derives it). Once supplied, the printed grid intersections themselves can register each relevant raster sheet to projected coordinates; the exact applicable sheets and their residuals/line-width would then be checked per cohort. No external modern control set is demanded. The map-source review confirms B11–16/Kotoriba for the 31 Zala keys only; it does not extend that sheet assignment to the other 37 Somogy keys, whose sheets remain unjoined. A later application must route those separately from the relevant B/C index before any per-key statement. Without that, a per-key map overlay would invent registration certainty.
