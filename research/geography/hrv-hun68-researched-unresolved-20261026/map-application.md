## Application work and result

I recomputed WGS84 center-coordinate extents from the exact 68 keys using the already-pinned WorldAtlas pixel-centre method (262,166 grid; no geometry input or producer run). The three bounded extents are: owner22086, 3 cells; owner22101, 34 cells; owner22186, 31 cells. No coordinates were transmitted externally. The index/map review establishes B-sector relevance at regional scale, especially the Kotoriba B11–16 sheets, but not a cell-to-sheet association at the required precision.

The earlier claim that the Laborde azimuth `alpha_c` was missing is withdrawn. The historical HÉR/HKR/HDR definition constructs the tangent-cylinder central great circle perpendicular to the Gellért meridian at the projection origin; that source geometry gives an eastward initial-line azimuth of 90° (270° for the reverse direction). This is a source-derived parameter, not a PROJ default inferred from the qualitative “SW” orientation. The [ELTE geodesy lecture](https://mercator.elte.hu/~gyorffy/jegyzete/alapfoga/heng_m2.html) states that the Gellért meridian is the straight x-axis and the perpendicular auxiliary great circle through each projection center is the true-scale y-axis; [ASPRS Grids & Datums, Hungary, p.15](https://www.asprs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/01-17-GD-Hungary.pdf) independently describes the three 1908 cylinders tangent along great circles perpendicular to the Budapest meridian. The base-map title page records the zonal-cylinder origin longitude as 36°42′53.5733″ east of Ferro. The 2003 technical paper explicitly says that its Greenwich longitude 19°02′56.9441″ was chosen arbitrarily and notes the original Ferro meridian; that paper value is therefore not itself an exact map-frame realization. The origin meridian/prime-meridian and axis conventions must be carried through the coordinate operation, rather than silently substituting a generic Greenwich parameter.

The printed rectangular map grids are source-grounded scan controls; the lack of GeoPDF metadata or latitude/longitude graticules does not prevent approximate sheet routing. I routed the exact 68 source-derived candidate coordinates to the relevant raster sheets under the documented candidate Laborde/frame interpretation, using the original atlas grid labels and printed scale (no historic/modern boundary geometry as a control). This is an exploratory application, not a validated coordinate transformation or final key-to-border adjudication.

- Drnje (owner 22086; 3 keys, Somogy): candidate points fall within B27's printed rectangle (H 159,400–160,400; V −76,100 to −75,500; scale 1:2,000). On a 70-dpi scan, the grid-frame calibration is about 0.73 m/pixel. Approximate center-to-red-line distances are 53.3 m, 24.0 m, and 77.3 m.
- Podravske Sesvete (owner 22101; 34 keys, Somogy): all candidate points fall within B70's printed rectangle (the retained scan is scale 1:5,000). The prior bounded raster pass measured approximate nearest-red-line distances of 0.9–462.9 m.
- Kotoriba (owner 22186; 31 keys, Zala): candidate routing spans B10 (27 keys) and B11 (4 keys), adjacent sheets at 1:5,000. Approximate nearest-red-line distances are 3.7–301.8 m on B10 and 130.6–442.0 m on B11.

These distances are descriptive measurements against the red line as rendered, not side classifications or thresholds. They omit unresolved effects from converting the original grid into the map's HD1909 frame, the published transformation's arbitrary Greenwich longitude and associated prime-meridian/rotation adjustment, the unreported local residual of the grid fit to each later-fitted scanned sheet, and the printed red-line width. Candidate-point locations close to the depicted line (including the 0.9 m minimum) are plainly indeterminate; even larger distances cannot be declared decisive absent a defensible bound for those combined uncertainties. The paper's national 2m mean/5m maximum datum-transform errors are not map-sheet registration errors and do not resolve this. No per-key side result is asserted, and I did not infer current sovereignty, whole-cell clearance, or legal ownership from which side a point might appear on.

The source defines the central-line azimuth: HÉR/HKR/HDR's central great circle is perpendicular to the Gellért meridian at the origin, yielding 90° eastward (270° reverse). This corrects the prior mistaken “missing alpha” statement. It does not resolve the remaining frame realization and scan-line uncertainty. The paper's transform parameters and arbitrary Greenwich longitude are useful candidate inputs, not a validated substitute for the map's original Ferro/frame conventions; the exact adjustment and sheet-local registration remain insufficiently bounded to classify these 68.
