A 2003 published technical paper on the Hungarian systems (pp.16–21; <https://real-j.mtak.hu/18365/9/GeK_2003_3.pdf>) documents Bessel 1841 / HD1909, the old zonal-cylindrical Oblique Mercator/Laborde family, the central/southern projection centers, Ferro convention, scale/orientation parameters, and a HD1909→WGS84 7-parameter transformation estimated from 66 national control points. The paper reports approximately 2m mean/5m maximum horizontal error for the seven-parameter transform in Hungary, but cautions that old map sheets with later-fitted grids may have local residuals requiring correction. Those are national datum-transform figures, not registration residuals for these scanned border sheets.

I checked the exact 68-key extents against the map series and inspected the actual scanned map marginalia. The atlas scans have no GeoPDF viewport/measure, embedded vectors, or latitude/longitude graticules, but their printed rectangular grid intersections can register the raster image to projected coordinates; a modern ground-control set is not a prerequisite for that part. The technical paper documents the Laborde projection family, center, scale/orientation description, prime-meridian discussion and HD1909→WGS84 transform, but it does not give the numeric azimuth (`alpha_c`) of the Laborde central line. The standard Laborde method requires that azimuth in addition to center/scale/origin (see [PROJ Laborde method documentation](https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/labrd.html)); PROJ’s default `+azi=0` is a software default, not a parameter identified by the source maps. The maps/paper give only qualitative “orientation SW,” which does not uniquely specify it. Pula's entries also do not give a complete coordinate sequence for each boundary segment. I therefore did not invent an axis angle or transform, digitize a purported point-to-line relationship, or infer a per-key side/contact. The specific remaining source-role link is the documented numeric projection-axis parameter (or authoritative equivalent) to convert current cell centers into the projected grid; then the printed grid supports scan residual fitting and line-width uncertainty measurement per relevant sheet. The B11–16/Kotoriba link is specific to that reach (31 Zala keys), not to the other 37 Somogy keys, whose map sheets remain unjoined.
