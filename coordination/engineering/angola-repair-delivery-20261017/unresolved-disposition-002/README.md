Bounded official border records confirm why those contacts are not resolved by the ADM1 names alone. Namibia's government Commission of Inquiry report describes the Angola–Namibia northern boundary as established by the 1886 German–Portuguese treaty, with a Ruacana-area uncertainty, and reports 1927–28 survey/geodetic demarcation ending in the Kakeri Declaration; Namibia's National Archives catalogues an `Angola Boundary Survey` file dated 1927–28 and separate Angola Boundary/Kunene Water Commission holdings. Zambia's Parliament records that the Angola–Zambia boundary was defined by the 18 November 1954 agreement and describes a section involving straight-line segments and the normal eastern limit of the Kwando when flooded; the UN Treaty Series contains the agreement and annexes. For the COD border, the Department of State's International Boundary Study 144 summarizes a line comprising rivers, pillars, straight segments, and drainage divides; it also reports that the 1891 convention left the exact Kwango–Kasai alignment to a later boundary commission, and the 1928 League of Nations Treaty Series separately records an Angola–Belgian Congo territorial exchange defined by the Kasai, Luakano, and Luao. For the COG border, the Department of State's International Boundary Study 105 describes the relevant boundary sector between named rivers and pillars, reproduces the 1886 France–Portugal convention and 1901 arrangement, and identifies historic boundary map series.

These records establish specific historical line rules, survey leads, and source-vintage differences—not an exact correspondence between the retained 2017/2022/2020 ADM1 feature boundaries and every held native centre.
