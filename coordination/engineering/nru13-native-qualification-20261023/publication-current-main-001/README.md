The 2021 census map offers general district context, but does not locate the held key against a boundary or identify the 2005 source vintage. The accessible public records reviewed so far do not resolve which representation applies to Aiwo.

An additional public comparison source is now identified: SPC Statistics for Development Division's [2011 Nauru PHC administrative-boundaries record](https://pacificdata.org/data/dataset/2011_nru_phc_admin_boundaries), specifically its `2011_PHC_NRU_District_4326` resource. The catalog describes 15 district Polygon/MultiPolygon features in WGS 84 (EPSG:4326), used for the 2011 Population and Housing Census; it lists Creative Commons Attribution, a 392.7 kB resource, and a June 29, 2020 catalog update. This is a concrete district-level source lead, but its catalog metadata alone does not establish the held center's district, the data's underlying survey/edit vintage, or continuity with the 2005 source. The browser reader could not render the linked GeoJSON download (HTTP 400 from the reader) and the PacificMap view returned HTTP 403; neither response establishes that the cataloged resource itself is access-restricted. No geometry was obtained or compared. The existing independent reviewer assessed this as a reasonable, still-unperformed point-specific comparison; a 2011 result would add historical context but would not prove 2005 lineage or current status.

The reasonable remaining step for Aiwo is to inspect and compare this cataloged district resource, or an authorized reusable PopGIS export, with a date, projection or calibration, and district crosswalk. Until such evidence is actually obtained and assessed, Aiwo remains held. This is a source-applicability limitation, not a sovereignty conclusion and not a claim that no other boundary record exists.
